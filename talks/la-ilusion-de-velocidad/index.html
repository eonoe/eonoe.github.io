The question was wrong. These are two different things.

Aviation already
solved this

FAA Safety Alert for Operators 13002 · 4 January 2013

“Continuous use of autoflight systems could lead to degradation of the pilot's ability to quickly recover the aircraft.”

They did not ban the autopilot. They mandated hand-flying.

The binding version is 14 CFR 121.423, Extended Envelope Training

“Manual flight is the foundation upon which other technical flying skills are built.”

FAA Advisory Circular 120-123, “Flightpath Management,” 2022 — still active

Replace “manual flight” with “writing and debugging code yourself” and read it again.

“The more time I spend working with coding agents, the more convinced I am that they make software engineering even harder. We can do amazing things with them, but unlocking their full potential requires extraordinary discipline and knowledge.”

Simon Willison, 24 September 2026

The most prominent optimist in this field, two weeks ago

Your manual flight hours

  1. Log your estimate before you start. Guess the time, then measure it. The gap is your calibration, and it is the only one you have.
  2. One task a week, by hand, on purpose. No agent. Pick something you would normally delegate.
  3. Review the plan, not the diff. A bad line is one bad line. A bad plan is two thousand.
  4. Before you merge, explain the mechanism out loud. If you cannot say why it works, you have not reviewed it. You have witnessed it.
  5. Debug without help once a week. It is the skill with the widest measured gap and the shortest shelf life.

Not a framework. Five habits, borrowed from an industry that had this problem first.
